Mobile App Compliance Audit

Tenna ELD driver app, screens D1 to D34 · 29 August 2026 · prepared by AppAxis

What this is. A check of the documented driver app against the federal ELD rules, looking for anything the rules require that the design does not yet cover.

What was compared. All 34 driver screens at the 9/2 revision, their data elements and their drawn content, against 49 CFR Part 395 and Appendix A to Subpart B. Section 3 of the proposal was checked alongside, so that a thing listed as a requirement but never drawn reads differently from a thing nobody has thought about.

What this is not. This reviews the documents, not code. It is also not a substitute for Tenna's FMCSA compliance advisor. Where a section number is marked verify we are confident about the requirement but not the exact citation, and we would rather say so than guess.

Findings that matter most

1. Hourly driving logs are required, and no screen shows them High

Appendix A, event type 2

When a truck is moving and the driver has not changed duty status for an hour, the ELD has to write a log entry on its own. These are called intermediate logs, and an inspector expects to see them in the record.

Section 3 of the proposal scopes them correctly: "Intermediate logs every 60 min while driving." They then appear nowhere else. No screen draws one, the data element table does not list them, and the ownership split does not say where they come from. The daily log (D8), the day detail (D10) and the officer view (D16) all show duty status changes only.

This is the single largest gap found: a whole required record type with no surface and no data element.

2. Trailer number and shipping document number are missing entirely High

Appendix A, required on the record and on the inspection display

The rules ask for the trailer number and the shipping document number, both on the log the officer reads and on the individual events. The driver has to be able to type them in.

Neither appears anywhere in the package. Not on a screen, not in the data element table, not in the ownership split. There is no entry surface for them and no place they are displayed.

3. The help screen tells drivers to use transfer methods the device will not have High

§395.22, carrier must supply a data transfer instruction sheet

The 8/28 revision settled that this ELD certifies as Telematics only: web services to eRODS with email as the fallback, and no USB or Bluetooth transfer to an inspector. D17 was changed to match and the peer to peer work was cut from the build.

The help screen (D25) was not changed. It still offers "Data transfer instructions · eRODS · email · Bluetooth · USB."

The carrier is required to give every driver an instruction sheet describing how this ELD transfers data. If that sheet describes two methods the device cannot perform, it is wrong, and a driver following it at the roadside would try something that cannot work. This is the cheapest fix on the list and the one with the most direct consequence.

4. Nothing handles the case where the app cannot get a location High

Appendix A, manual location entry verify

When the ELD cannot work out where the truck is, it has to ask the driver to type the location in, and it has to record how far the truck has travelled since the last good position reading.

Neither exists in the package. There is no manual location entry, no mention of a location description field being entered by hand, and no "distance since last valid coordinates."

This is not a theoretical gap here. The app's only source of position is the tracker over Bluetooth, and the package already documents two states where that gives nothing: Q3 records that the tracker returns an empty VIN and a zero odometer with the ignition off, and Q1 and D19 cover the link dropping while the driver walks away with the phone. Those are exactly the conditions this requirement exists for.

5. The annotation rule is cited to the wrong section, and the number looks wrong too High verify

Edits and annotations sit under §395.30, not §395.32

Five places in the package state that a note of at least 8 characters is required when a driver sets or edits a status by hand. Three of them attribute it to §395.32:

  • D7: "The note of 8 characters or more that §395.32 requires…"
  • Section 3: "8+ character note when status is manually edited. Required by §395.32."

Two separate problems. First, §395.32 is the section about unidentified driving records. Edits, certification and annotations are §395.30, which D12's own screen cites correctly, so the package contradicts itself. Second, our understanding is that the minimum length in Appendix A is 4 characters, not 8, with a separate 5 character minimum for a location typed in by hand.

Asking for 8 is allowed and is arguably better practice. Describing 8 as what the regulation requires is a factual error in the document that gates certification. Both the section number and the number of characters need the advisor to confirm them. We would fix this one first: it is quick, and it is wrong in the direction that looks careless rather than cautious.

6. Event rows on the officer's screen have no location Medium

D16 lists each event as sequence, time, status, engine hours, vehicle miles and origin. The driver's own screens (D8 and D10) show a place name such as "Blue Ash, OH" but the officer's screen does not. Location belongs on every event.

7. The malfunction indicator does not reach the inspector Medium

D18 interrupts the driver and D19 shows a banner, so the driver always knows. An inspector reading D16 sees nothing. The indicator has to be visible on the screen handed over at the roadside.

Other gaps

8. A driver cannot claim an hours of service exception Medium

§395.1, adverse driving conditions and the short haul exception

Section 3 scopes the engine to handle "adverse driving conditions, the short-haul air-mile exemption, agricultural" exemptions. No screen lets a driver say they are using one. D6 offers Personal Conveyance and Yard Move and nothing else. Every other use of the word "exception" in the wireframes is about screen navigation.

A driver taking the two extra hours for bad weather has to be able to say so, and it has to land on the record.

9. The app stays fully usable while the truck is moving Medium

§392.80 and §392.82, texting and handheld phone use while driving

D5 says automatic driving detection cannot be overridden while the vehicle is moving, but that is an anti fraud control rather than a safety one. Nothing anywhere locks the interface down at speed.

Handheld phone use and texting while driving a commercial vehicle are prohibited, and can disqualify the driver and penalise the carrier. Commercial ELD apps normally disable most of the interface above a few miles an hour. D29, the stationary prompt, is fine by design because it only appears when stopped. Nothing else is constrained.

10. Personal Conveyance location precision is not addressed Medium verify

Position is recorded less precisely during Personal Conveyance, roughly to the nearest ten miles, so that a driver's personal trips are not tracked in detail. Nothing in the package mentions reduced precision. D6 records Personal Conveyance with no note that its position handling differs.

11. Special duty modes may need to reset when the engine cycles Medium verify

D6 says "Personal conveyance ends automatically if the vehicle re-enters commercial use." Our understanding is that the rule is stricter: the special mode clears when the engine is switched off and on, and the driver has to choose it again. As drawn, the screen may be more permissive than the rule allows.

12. A driver cannot get a copy of their own logs Low verify

D17 sends records to FMCSA at the roadside. W7 and W13 export for the carrier. Nothing lets a driver take a copy of their own record away with them, which the carrier is obliged to provide on request.

What is already right

Most of the app holds up, and the parts that are hardest to get right are the parts that are right.

  • The four duty statuses, the note required on a manual change, and the automatic switch after five minutes stopped with sixty seconds to answer (D29), including recording whether the change was automatic or the driver's. That last detail is what auditors actually check.
  • Automatic driving time cannot be edited, originals are kept, and a carrier cannot change a log without the driver agreeing (D12, D13, D20, D21). The edit history faults FMCSA cites most often are designed out.
  • Daily certification using the regulation's own wording, and re-certification after an edit (D11).
  • Unidentified driving: eight days on the phone, six months on the host, annotate or assign, and the assignment coming back to the driver to accept (D22, D28, D34).
  • Malfunctions separated from diagnostics by severity, the eight day clock, paper logs starting and stopping (D18, D19, D32).
  • One transfer family only, with no peer to peer path in the build (D17).
  • The PIN in and out of inspection mode (D15, D31).
