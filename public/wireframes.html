Revision 8/28/2026

What changed, and why.

On August 17 Tenna's FMCSA compliance advisor reviewed the package specifically for host reporting and sent back a memo. Four of its points change screens; two add them. Everything below is marked in place and outlined, so the edits can be found without hunting. The control at the bottom right switches between the original and this revision. Nothing was deleted from the original; it is still readable in full.

D22

Unidentified driving shrinks on the phone. The memo asked why D22 looked like it was "trying to do it all at the mobile." The in-cab device only has to carry the last 8 days of unaccepted records; review, annotation, and assignment are the carrier's legal duty. D22 is now an 8-day claim/reject list with a reason picker (D28), and the real work moves to a new host report (W16).

W16

New: the host unidentified-driving report. The memo's central ask. A working queue with the suggested-reason pop-ups, 6-month retention, and a review-cadence nudge, because a carrier that does not work it two or three times a week loses control of it. This also closes the inventory gap the original page flagged for Thursday.

D17

One transfer family, not both. Per the memo's note on D17 of the specification: a device does telematics transfer (web services or email) or peer-to-peer (Bluetooth / USB), never both. The Bluetooth·USB option is gone from the driver screen and from the native-module scope.

D16

Three fields added to the inspection display. Sequence number, elapsed engine hours, and accumulated vehicle miles, per §4.5.1.1. FMCSA's own example omits them; the memo's experience is that including them is better received. The same fields drive the new host inspector view (W18).

D27

New: a non-HOS move stamp. The memo lists the four ordinary causes of unidentified driving (mechanic road test, yard movement, OEM warranty movement, yard relocation), and notes that carriers try to cover them with a shared "exempt driver" account that is hard to enforce. D27 lets a named mechanic or yard hand stamp the move in two taps, so the record never becomes unidentified in the first place.

W17 · W18

New: host eRODS transfer and host inspector mode. FMCSA now requests eRODS transfers days before an on-site audit, for named drivers over up to 30 days. W17 does that from the back office and refuses to push six months at once. W18 is the internal-audit log view the memo recommends.

Also changed: D2 keeps pairing sticky so short-haul drivers stop generating unidentified records by never signing in; D6 drops the shared-exempt-driver assumption; D9 settles at 8 days on the phone with longer history staying on the host; W13 carries a new open question about whether the audit package is over-scoped; W15 gains the reason taxonomy, the review-cadence reminder, and non-HOS move roles. The data element table and the flow map pick up the new surfaces.

Not changed, deliberately: the five product decisions. Nothing in the memo settles P1–P5, and the new host screens do not depend on them except where tagged.

P1

Are drivers full Tenna users, or do they get a simpler ELD-only login?

Luke called this the big one: most of the interface design depends on it. It decides where each driver's record lives, what the login screen (D1) asks for, and what the profile screen (D23) shows.

A · Full Tenna users Drivers are regular Tenna users. Their identity, profile, and permissions live in Tenna, and the ELD reads them through the API. One list of people, managed in one place.
B · ELD-only driver login The ELD keeps its own simple driver records, and Tenna only sees drivers where it needs to. Quicker to build, but two lists of people to keep in sync.

P3

Does Tenna keep the raw tracker data, and if so, where?

The second-by-second readings from the tracker (position, speed, odometer) won't go through Tenna's existing data pipeline. So: does Tenna need to keep those raw readings at all? And if yes, does Tenna store and process them, or does the ELD hold them?

B · Tenna keeps the raw data The raw readings flow into Tenna and are stored alongside its other asset data, through a new connection built for this.

"Host" means the back end, not a screen. The memo's language is the regulation's: an ELD is two halves. The in-vehicle ELD is the phone plus the tracker: it records events, holds a short working window (8 days of logs, 8 days of unaccepted unidentified driving), and shows the record at roadside. The host is the motor carrier's back-end system: the server that holds the full 6-month record, and the web surfaces over it. So every W screen on this page is a host surface, and "host reporting" means reports the carrier runs against that server. When the memo says something belongs on the host rather than the mobile, it is a statement about which system owns the data and the legal duty, not about which screen is prettier.

The ownership split

Ownership and API Surfaces

A draft of which system supplies and owns each piece of data. The ground rule is Tamar’s: “you’ll have your own schema in the database, your own APIs, and you’ll structure them separately”, with shared users the exception. Nothing in this section requires access to Tenna’s schema.

Axis one

Origin

Where the value is first produced: measured by the tracker, stored in Tenna, typed by a person, or computed by the ELD. Origin says nothing about who stores the result.

Tenna platform Tracker Entered Derived

Entered covers anything a person types on one of these screens: driver, mechanic, or back-office supervisor.

Axis two

System of record

Which system is the authoritative store: it keeps the value and produces it in an audit. A value can come from the tracker and still be owned by the ELD; several are.

Tenna ELD Open

Earlier revisions of this page used one tag for both questions. That is why four rows used to read "Tenna API and ELD-owned" without saying which system owned what.

Four rules behind the split

  1. Tenna IDs are the join keys. The ELD stores a reference and reads the record when it needs to display it. It never copies a Tenna-owned record into its own schema, so there is no second list of people to keep in sync. Tamar, 28 August: the users are shared, the rest is on your schema, and the ID is what identifies it.
  2. On-demand reads, not bulk sync. Screens fetch data when they need it. Nothing is copied on a schedule, so the ELD never holds a stale copy of a person or an asset. Working assumption from the 13 August kickoff.
  3. New, purpose-built APIs rather than direct access to Tenna's existing ones. Every T surface below would be built for this integration and scoped to what the ELD needs. Working assumption from the 13 August kickoff.
  4. The certifiable record stays in the ELD's system of record. Tenna can receive copies, and for events it should. But the log an inspector reads, its edit history, and the output files generated from it all live in one system. This is why S1 through S4 are separate domains.
