Phase 2 Deliverable · Certification Readiness

The road to a defensible self-certification.

Luke asked for this at the close of Phase 2: a certification-readiness roadmap that defines what has to be decided, what has to be tested, and what evidence has to exist so that Tenna's self-certification signature is safe — and stays safe under audit. This page is that roadmap: six stages, each with an owner, an exit gate, and the artifact that proves the gate was passed. It extends Section 8 of the proposal, which explains how FMCSA registration actually works.

Draft · 3 September 2026 · for review with Tenna's FMCSA compliance advisors

Nothing on this page is agreed yet. It exists to be corrected in the room: the stages, the gate criteria, and especially the registration mechanics in Stage F are AppAxis's reading of the regulation and of the advisors' 17 August memo, written down so the experts have something concrete to mark up. Items that need their answer are collected at the bottom.

The Shape of It

Self-certification is easy. Staying certified is the work.

FMCSA's ELD registration has no third-party lab and no gatekeeper: a provider self-certifies on a web form and the device appears on the public list within days. The discipline is surviving what comes later — an audit triggered by a complaint or a random pull, sometimes months out. Roughly forty devices have been revoked to date, twelve in May 2026 alone, and the failure modes repeat (mapped below).

The division of labor was set in the June 4 session and holds throughout: Tenna's FMCSA compliance expert owns the documentation package, the public user guide, the Equipment Registration submission, and the relationship with FMCSA. AppAxis owns certification readiness: a device, a test suite, and an evidence trail that make the signature defensible. Every stage below carries that split explicitly.

The Roadmap

Six stages, each with a gate you can point at.

Stages A and B run now, inside Phase 2. Stages C through F track the phase plan (Section 7): build in Phase 3, transfer and host surfaces in Phase 4, verification and registration in Phase 5, and the treadmill after listing. A stage is done when its gate statement is true and its evidence artifact exists — not before.

STAGE A

Hardware validation — prove the telemetry the ELD will be built on

In flight · Phase 2 AppAxis

Everything downstream assumes the tracker's Bluetooth broadcast can carry the four contract elements (engine power, motion, miles, engine hours) reliably: through backgrounding, signal loss, process death, and multi-day operation. That is what the Phase 2 prototype and the bench/drive protocol exist to prove — on real hardware, with every run leaving an append-only on-device session log as evidence.

  • Test inventory: Tenna's original 15 connectivity cases (Mark's sheet, wording unchanged) plus the AppAxis-added scenarios 16–31 — clock integrity, airplane-mode drives, 24-hour soak, stored-event drain/purge semantics, process death and gap coverage, the 30-minute §395.34 malfunction escalation, and automatic duty-status transitions on the road.
  • Acceptance criteria are written against 49 CFR Part 395 and Appendix A — and are still AppAxis proposals, not agreed criteria. Ratifying them is on the advisor agenda.
  • Status: the Android re-certification pass stands at 23 of 26 protocol steps fully green, with the remainder staged to close on one scheduled drive. The iOS pass follows the same protocol.
Gate A Every protocol step green against advisor-ratified acceptance criteria, on both platforms, with the findings write-up delivered. Evidence: the completed test workbooks and the per-test session logs pulled from the devices.
STAGE B

Compliance requirements freeze — settle what the regulation means before building to it

In flight · Phase 2 exit Joint · advisors decide, AppAxis drafts, Tenna ratifies

The wireframes now cover all 37 MUST requirements (the coverage table), and the 17 August compliance review is folded in. What remains are the interpretation calls that only the advisors can make — each one currently blocks either a data model or a screen from being final. This stage is the scope-lock deliverable the contract names: "the certification-readiness plan with Tenna's FMCSA compliance expert. Both sides sign before any production code is written."

  • P3 — raw telemetry retention (decision log): what an audit actually requires, and whether device-local retention satisfies it.
  • Q1 — duty status when the phone loses the vehicle link (the four sub-questions): the deepest open behavior question, blocking the Phase 3 HOS engine spec.
  • Q15–Q17 — unidentified driving rulings: the non-HOS move stamp's event class, whether rules-based auto-annotation is defensible, and what "unaccepted" and the 6-month retention unit mean.
  • Q18 — the Telematics transfer election, formally signed: it is a one-way self-certification declaration.
  • Q19 / Q20 — audit export scope and the host inspector view: scope calls with compliance consequences.
  • D29 — stationary prompt parameters confirmed (window, timeout, recorded origin) before the production state machine is specified.
Gate B Scope lock signed: every item above carries a written advisor answer in the decision log, the final HOS engine spec and data-retention design reflect them, and acceptance criteria are ratified. Evidence: the signed scope-lock package and the updated decision log.
STAGE C

Build to Appendix A — with the test harness growing alongside the code

Phase 3 · weeks 4–12 AppAxis

The core build: the HOS state machine covering every duty rule and exception, the malfunction and diagnostic engine across all 8 categories of §395.34(a), the FMCSA-format output file generator with its line check, and the driver application on real hardware. The certification-critical discipline is that the QA engineer builds the specification-driven test harness in the same weeks — every Appendix A behavior carries a test from the day it is implemented, because that harness is the backbone of the readiness dossier in Stage E.

  • Every event type in Appendix A's Table 6, every malfunction/diagnostic trigger and clear condition, every auto-transition (including the D29 stationary prompt) — implemented and tested together.
  • Output files validated continuously against FMCSA's published file specification and reference validator, not once at the end.
  • Weekly Friday showbacks so interpretation drift is caught in days, not phases.
Gate C The Appendix A harness is green for every section implemented, and an output file generated from live test data validates clean. Evidence: the harness run report per release.
STAGE D

Transfer and host surfaces — the parts an inspector and an auditor actually touch

Phase 4 · weeks 13–15 AppAxis

The data-transfer service to eRODS (web services with email fallback, per the Q18 election) with both of its callers — the driver at roadside (D17) and the back office for pre-audit requests (W17, with the 30-day-per-transfer cap) — plus the host reporting surfaces the 17 August review asked for: the unidentified-driving report (W16) and, pending Q20, the host inspector view (W18).

  • Transfer verified against FMCSA's eRODS test environment before any pilot driver meets an officer.
  • The unidentified-driving loop closed end to end: tracker capture → 8-day in-cab list → host review → annotate or assign → driver confirmation (D34) → 6-month retention.
  • Edit-and-annotation versioning demonstrated: original preserved, attribution complete, trail rendering in both the driver and host views.
Gate D End-to-end system on a test fleet; a roadside transfer and a host-initiated transfer both accepted by the eRODS test endpoint. Evidence: transfer confirmations and the test-fleet session record.
STAGE E

Certification-readiness verification — assemble the dossier that survives an audit

Phase 5 · weeks 16–17 AppAxis assembles · advisors accept

Before Tenna signs anything, the whole case is put in one place and reviewed by the compliance expert as if they were the auditor. This is the concrete exit criterion for AppAxis's side of the engagement.

  • Full Appendix A test suite passing on the integrated system — every event type, every malfunction trigger, every diagnostic threshold — with results archived per release.
  • Output-file validation results, including the edge cases that most often revoke devices: edit history, driver–vehicle pairings, multi-driver scenarios.
  • The malfunction and diagnostic evidence set: each of the 8 categories provoked, detected, displayed, and cleared on record.
  • The on-road scenario protocol re-run on the customer device matrix Ross provided, on release builds.
  • The in-cab documentation §395.22(g)–(h) requires the driver to carry: user's manual, data-transfer instruction sheet, malfunction instruction sheet, and a supply of blank paper logs — drafted by AppAxis, owned and published by Tenna.
  • The public user-instructions page (required by the registration form, accessible without login) — content ready for Tenna's URL.
Gate E Tenna's compliance expert accepts the dossier as sufficient to sign. Evidence: the dossier itself and the expert's written acceptance.
STAGE F

Registration, listing, and the treadmill after

Phase 5 · weeks 17–19, then ongoing Tenna · AppAxis supporting

Tenna's compliance expert files the Equipment Registration: provider info, the unique 4-character ELD identifier, model name and software version, the operating-systems list (each OS bundle registers separately, on our current reading — a specific item to verify with the advisors), the Telematics election from Q18, the self-certification statement, the public instructions URL, and the public contact. Devices typically appear on the list within 1–5 business days. AppAxis supports the pilot in parallel and fixes anything the certification pass surfaces.

  • After listing, the risk window opens: audits arrive via complaint or random pull, sometimes months out. The Stage E dossier is what answers them.
  • The ongoing treadmill, Tenna-owned with AppAxis available for the code it generates: FMCSA interpretation updates, re-submission on major versions, app-store re-reviews, driver-reported edge cases, the annual user-guide refresh.
Gate F Device listed on the FMCSA registry; the stay-listed runbook (who watches what, who responds to what, on what clock) handed to Tenna and staffed. Evidence: the public listing and the runbook.

For the Advisors

What this draft needs from the compliance review.

Each item below either blocks a gate or shapes one. The full background for the numbered questions lives in the decision log; they are gathered here so the review can walk the list top to bottom.

P3

Raw telemetry retention — what does an audit actually require?

Does an FMCSA audit require the raw pre-processing telemetry to substantiate ELD events, or only the distilled event records? If raw data must be kept: which elements, at what granularity, for how long, and is device-local retention acceptable? The answer decides the data pipeline (P3) and goes to Tamar for Luke's decision.

Q1

Duty status when the phone loses the vehicle link.

The four sub-questions on record (Q1): which component is the ELD of record; may the tracker's stored stop/start attestations be applied retroactively on reconnect; if not, what is the correct behavior; and is this a §4.6 malfunction matter rather than a duty-status one. Blocks the Phase 3 HOS engine spec.

Q15–Q17

The unidentified-driving rulings.

The non-HOS move stamp's event class and whether it discharges the review duty (Q15); whether rules-based auto-annotation is defensible at all (Q16); what "unaccepted" means and how the 6-month retention clock is scoped (Q17). These decide the W16/D22/D28/D34 data model.

Q18

The Telematics election, formally.

Confirmation that Telematics (web services + email) is the right election for this fleet, a walk-through of the no-coverage roadside case now that the thumb drive is gone, and the election signed before Phase 3 (Q18). One-way door.

Q19 · Q20

Audit export scope, and the host inspector view.

The W13 line-by-line walk-through the memo asked for (Q19), and the build/cut call on W18 with our recommendation to build it (Q20).

AC

Ratify the acceptance criteria.

The bench and drive protocol's acceptance criteria are AppAxis proposals inferred from Part 395 and Appendix A, marked as such on the sheet. Gate A cannot close on self-graded criteria: the advisors ratify, correct, or extend them — including naming any scenario the inventory still lacks.

Stage F

Verify the registration mechanics.

Specific claims to confirm or correct with the people who have filed these: per-OS registration bundles, the 4-character ELD identifier's constraints, what version-string discipline avoids needless re-submission, what the public instructions URL must contain, and who inside Tenna signs the self-certification statement with what diligence behind it.

Why These Gates

Every known revocation trigger, mapped to the stage that answers it.

The roadmap is shaped backwards from how devices actually get revoked. Each failure mode from the recent revocation rounds pairs with the stage and artifact built to prevent it.

Output file format errors

Missing fields, wrong line check, wrong ordering, encoding mistakes. Answered by: Stage C's continuous validation and Stage E's per-release validation results — never a one-time check.

Malfunction handling gaps

A missed §395.34(a) category, or a clear that never happens. Answered by: Stage C's engine built with its tests, D32 making the clear visible, and Stage E's provoked-and-cleared evidence set.

Engine sync failures

ECM data not polled at cadence, or sync claimed after it was lost. Answered by: Stage A — the cadence watchdog, loss detection, and the 30-minute escalation are already tested on hardware.

Edit history gaps

Edits unannotated, attribution missing, originals lost. Answered by: Stage C/D versioning with the original preserved permanently, and the edit-history edge cases in Stage E's validation set.

Unidentified driving not handled

Unlogged driving not captured, or captured and never reviewed. Answered by: the tracker's store-and-forward (Stage A), the W16 review loop (Stage D), and the Q15–Q17 rulings (Stage B) so the model is right before it is built.

Driver complaints

Impossible logs, dropped events, transfers that fail at roadside. Answered by: Stage D's eRODS test-environment verification and Stage F's pilot, where real drivers hit it before the public does.
