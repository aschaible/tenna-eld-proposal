A Field Guide · Compiled From tenna.com

Tenna isn't an ELD vendor.
It's an equipment operating system
for construction contractors.

Tenna sells one platform, TennaCORE, that consolidates everything a construction company needs to know about its physical assets: where they are, who's using them, whether they're broken, and whether the people operating them are compliant. ELD is the exception: a separate product that integrates with the platform rather than a tab inside it. Understanding the rest is how you understand what a new ELD product would actually plug into.

What Tenna Does

The 30-second version.

Tenna sells construction contractors a single platform plus a family of trackers that physically attach to every asset in a mixed fleet, from a $1.2M crawler crane to a $20 angle grinder, and feeds everything back into one web app and one mobile app.

Who they sell to.

Construction contractors with mixed fleets: heavy iron, on-road trucks, mid-sized equipment, attachments, tools, and consumables. Mid-market companies that have outgrown spreadsheets and whiteboards but aren't ready for SAP.

What they replace.

Six different point tools: a GPS tracker, a CMMS for maintenance, a dispatch board, an inspection app, a dash cam vendor, and a fuel/IFTA tool. Tenna pitches "one platform" instead of stitching those together.

How they're priced.

Hardware (the trackers, owned rather than leased), plus a SaaS base (TennaCORE), plus optional Premium Product modules. Unlimited users. SOC 2 Type 1. Mobile + web.

How they differentiate.

Construction-native. Motive and Samsara treat construction as one vertical of many; Tenna treats it as the only vertical. Workflows, terminology, and edge cases (yard moves, attachments, idle yellow iron) are built in rather than configured around.

The Modules · In Detail

What each piece actually does.

Tenna calls the base platform TennaCORE and everything else "Tenna Premium Products." Below is what's inside each one, described in terms of the job it does for a contractor, not in marketing language.

CORE

TennaCORE

THE BASE PLATFORM · ALWAYS INCLUDED

The system of record. Every asset, every site, every user, every map view, every report. If you only buy one thing from Tenna, you buy this.

  • Asset records: classification, ownership, serial numbers, warranties, manuals, attachments, rentals, consumables.
  • Master Map: all assets, sites, geofences in one view. Live and historical trip data.
  • Sites & Geofences: projects, yards, custom zones with enter/exit alerts.
  • Utilization: engine hours, mileage, idle vs. active, PTO, ECU mileage, project-level use.
  • Analytics & Reporting: dashboards, customizable reports, the "Answers" Q&A surface.
  • Tenna App (mobile): iOS + Android, offline mode, QR scanning, photos, asset requests.
  • Org Chart + Data Access Control: mirror complex subsidiary structures with per-user permissions.
MX

Maintenance (CMMS)

PREMIUM MODULE

A full computerized maintenance management system built into the platform, so shop work links directly to the asset record, not a separate app.

  • Preventive Maintenance: usage- or time-based triggers, auto-generated work requests.
  • Work Orders: create, track, bill; attach photos, parts, labor, cost codes.
  • Parts Inventory: per-location stock, restock alerts, link parts to service tasks.
  • Mechanic Time Cards: log wrench-time against equipment/job/cost code.
  • Construction Cost Codes: accurate job costing for labor + materials.
  • Fault Codes / DTC: issues flagged by the asset's ECU or OEM telematics flow into the shop queue automatically.
S&C

Safety & Compliance

PREMIUM MODULE

The regulatory and behavioral layer. It keeps DOT off the company's back and creates a documentation trail when something goes wrong.

  • AI Dash Cam (TennaCAM 2.0): driver-facing video, event detection, driver coaching evidence.
  • Driver Scorecard: speeding, harsh braking, idling, rolling up to a per-driver grade.
  • Operations / Safety Scorecard: equipment-side equivalent.
  • Custom Inspections: define checklists, schedule them, see what's missed.
  • DVIR: Driver Vehicle Inspection Reports, mobile.
  • IFTA: interstate fuel tax reporting.
  • ELD: Hours of Service. A separate product, not part of this module. Today white-labeled from a partner; the product Jose wants to bring in-house. Listed here because it pairs tightly with DVIR, IFTA, and the scorecards.
RM

Resource Management & Dispatching

PREMIUM MODULE

The "what's going where on Monday" layer. Connects the asset list to the job schedule.

  • Requests: field crews ask for equipment or maintenance attention; office plans reservations.
  • Dispatch: coordinate moves between sites, manage logistics.
  • Labor & Crews: driver schedules, dispatch events.
  • Master Schedule: single calendar view that pulls Requests, Work Orders, Dispatch, and more.
INT

Integrations

PREMIUM MODULE

The connectors that keep Tenna from becoming a 13th system to reconcile every Monday. Tenna's API + named integrations.

  • Accounting / ERP: push asset costs, labor, parts to the books.
  • Project Management / Construction Software: link assets to projects in the systems estimators already use.
  • OEM Telematics & AEMP: pull data from Cat, Komatsu, Volvo, etc. so OEM-equipped iron shows up in Tenna without a Tenna tracker.
TM

Tracker Management

UTILITY MODULE

The fleet-of-trackers admin view: provisioning, replacement, inventory of Tenna devices themselves. Boring but load-bearing for any contractor with 500+ trackers.

  • Per-device status, firmware, install location.
  • Bulk assign / reassign trackers to assets.
  • Implementation support workflows.

How It All Connects

The mental model that ties it together.

None of these modules are interesting in isolation. The reason contractors actually buy Tenna is because of the joins between them: what happens when a tracker, an asset record, a maintenance trigger, and a job schedule are all on the same backend. Four examples:

Tracker → Maintenance trigger
TennaCANbus → ECU hours → TennaCORE → PM rule fires → Work Order created

A CAN-bus tracker on an excavator reports 250 engine hours. TennaCORE's Maintenance module's preventive rule fires automatically and a work order lands on the shop's screen with the right parts and cost code pre-attached.

Driver → Scorecard → Coaching
TennaCAM Fleet → harsh-brake event → Driver Scorecard → Coaching workflow

An AI dash cam captures a harsh-braking event. The Driver Scorecard updates that driver's grade. The Safety & Compliance module flags them for a coaching session automatically, on the same platform an in-house ELD would feed HOS events into.

Geofence → Utilization → Job cost
Geofence entered → site assignment → utilization tagged → ERP push

A loader enters the geofence around Project 4521. TennaCORE auto-tags utilization to that project. The Integrations module pushes the hours and cost codes into the contractor's ERP, with no superintendent paperwork in between.

Field request → Dispatch → Asset move
Field crew scans TennaQR → Request submitted → Dispatch reroutes → Asset transferred

Field crew scans a QR tag on a missing tool, submits a request. Resource Management sees nearby assets via the Master Map and reroutes a delivery. Master Schedule updates. The asset's record reflects the new site assignment.

The Take · Why This Matters For An ELD Build

The ELD is a standalone product. The ecosystem is what it plugs into.

The single most important thing to understand about Tenna's product surface is how much every module shares: the same asset records, the same map, the same mobile app, the same user permissions, the same ECM data feed from the same trackers. The ELD is the deliberate exception. Tenna has been clear that it's a separate product, with its own backend, its own driver app, and its own fleet portal, not a tab inside the platform.

That separation is what makes bringing it in-house interesting. Built generically, an ELD is a commodity; Motive and Samsara already do it well. Built as a standalone product integrated at the core, consuming the TennaFLEET ECM feed, the asset record, the driver profile, and Tenna SSO, and pushing HOS events back to the scorecard and dispatch board, it becomes something the big general-purpose ELDs structurally cannot match. The ecosystem above isn't the container the ELD lives in. It's the integration surface that makes a Tenna ELD worth building.
