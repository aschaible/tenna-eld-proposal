One

The Driver’s Day

M. Alvarez, Unit 218, a Tuesday. Each step names the screen, what the driver does, and what the ELD writes down. Three events interrupt the day rather than following the sequence; each is marked where it happens.

Before the wheel turns
05:40

Sign in

D1D3

The driverTypes Tenna credentials into the app, or on a returning personal device looks at it and Face ID releases the saved session. On a team run the co-driver types a password behind them (D3): a device enrols one face.

What the ELD recordsA login event with the driver ID that stamps every event for the rest of the shift. The biometric never leaves the phone, and Cognito sees a token refresh rather than a person, which is what Q24 is about.

05:44

Pair the truck

D2

The driverPicks Unit 218 out of the trackers in range. Pairing is required before any duty status can change.

What the ELD recordsA pairing event tying this driver to this vehicle. The VIN stays unconfirmed until the engine runs, so the first read confirms the truck rather than naming it.

05:50

Go on duty

D5D7

The driverTaps ON and writes the note the rule requires, at least 8 characters.

What the ELD recordsA duty-status event with time, position to within a mile, driver ID, vehicle ID, engine power, motion state, miles and engine hours. The note is attached to it.

06:05

Pre-trip inspection

D14

The driverLeaves for Tenna’s DVIR flow and comes back to D4 when it is done.

What the ELD recordsNothing. §396.11 belongs to Tenna’s existing module; the ELD tracks only whether the inspection is required, started or submitted.

On the road
06:20

Driving starts by itself

D4

The driverNothing. Past 5 mph the ELD sets Driving whatever the driver last selected.

What the ELD recordsAn automatic duty-status event. This is the specification’s main anti-fraud control, and it cannot be overridden while the tracker sees motion.

09:15

Five minutes stopped

D29

The driverAnswers the prompt, or dismisses it for a long traffic signal, or does neither.

What the ELD recordsThe prompt, the answer, and after 60 seconds of silence an automatic switch to ON. All three, because the origin of the event is what an auditor reads.

10:30

Move the truck inside the yard

D6

The driverSelects Yard Move with a reason, before the truck moves.

What the ELD recordsMotion recorded under ON but exempt from the drive clock. Whether this driver may do it at all is carrier policy, set on W15.

12:00

Hand over the wheel

D33

The driverOn a team run, marks the co-driver as the one at the wheel.

What the ELD recordsThe swap, with both drivers named. Driving time before this moment stays with the outgoing driver and nothing already recorded moves.

Three things that interrupt the day
Branch

An officer pulls the truck over

D15D16D17D31

The driverEnters the PIN, hands the phone over, and takes it back with the same PIN when the officer is done.

What the ELD recordsThe 8-day record in FMCSA format with the §4.5.1.1 fields on every row, the eRODS transfer with the officer’s code in the file comment, and the attempt itself. Failures are recorded too, and all of it lands on W14.

Branch

The ELD malfunctions

D18D32

The driverAcknowledges a notice that cannot be swiped away, then keeps paper logs until told to stop.

What the ELD recordsThe malfunction with its code and its 8-day clock, the acknowledgment, and later the clear event and the paper-log period an auditor will ask for.

Branch

The carrier proposes an edit

D20D21

The driverReads the original beside the proposal and approves or rejects it, with a reason if rejecting.

What the ELD recordsThe decision, either way. The carrier cannot change a log on its own, and that requirement defines the workflow.

End of the day
16:40

Answer for driving nobody claimed

D22D28

The driverWorks the last 8 days of unaccepted records. Two questions only: is this mine, and if not, what was it.

What the ELD recordsThe claim, or the rejection with its reason. Review, annotation and assignment are the carrier’s duty under §395.32, and they happen on W16.

Days later

The carrier sends one back

D34

The driverGets a push saying the carrier attributed an Aug 19 yard move to them, and accepts or rejects it.

What the ELD recordsThe acceptance, which writes 8 minutes of ON into a day already certified, or the rejection with the driver’s reason. §395.32(a) lets a carrier annotate or assign, and the assign leg is the one that changes somebody’s log, so it cannot be silent.

17:50

Read the day and fix what is wrong

D8D10D12D13

The driverReads the grid, opens a segment, corrects it with the required note.

What the ELD recordsA new event linked to the original, which is kept permanently. Automatic driving time cannot be changed at all.

18:00

Certify

D9D11

The driverSigns the regulation’s exact statement for today, and for anything still uncertified behind it.

What the ELD recordsA certification event with driver ID and timestamp, for each day signed.

18:05

Log out

D30

The driverClears the uncertified days, decides whether to release Unit 218, and signs out.

What the ELD recordsA logout event. After it, any movement of this truck is unidentified driving until someone signs in, which is exactly what D22 and W16 exist to clean up.

Two

The Supervisor’s Day

The same day from the back office. It starts with the calls that prevent violations and ends with audit preparation for when FMCSA writes ahead. W1 to W4 is the main path; everything else is reached from the module nav.

07:30

The board

W1W2

The driverReads who is driving, what is left on each clock, and who is closest to a violation.

What the ELD recordsNothing new. This surface exists for the phone call that prevents the violation, which is why it sorts by urgency rather than by name.

08:15

What broke overnight

W8W9W10

The driverWorks the violation queue and the malfunction queue.

What the ELD recordsA remediation note against each violation, and the 8-day clock per device. An expiring clock nobody sees is how devices reach FMCSA’s revocation list.

09:00

Unidentified driving

W16

The driverTwo or three times a week, not daily. Annotates each open record with a reason, or assigns it to a driver.

What the ELD recordsThe annotation or the assignment, kept six months. Assigning pushes the record to that driver on D34 for their answer; annotating does not. A carrier that lets this slide loses control of it.

10:00

A log that looks wrong

W3W4W5W6

The driverBrowses to the day, reads it, proposes a correction, and tracks what the driver did with it.

What the ELD recordsA proposal, never a change. It reaches the driver on D20, is decided on D21, and comes back to W6.

13:00

The fleet and its devices

W11W12

The driverChecks which tracker is in which truck and whether the mapping can be trusted.

What the ELD recordsThe mapping. The VIN cannot confirm it from the back office, so the check runs on the vehicle’s first drive instead.

15:00

Policy

W15

The driverSets cycles, special-duty permissions, warning thresholds, yard fences, the reason list, the review cadence and which roles may stamp a non-HOS move.

What the ELD recordsCarrier policy. Every setting here configures the compliance engine; none of them can turn a requirement off.

Audit

When FMCSA writes ahead

W17W18W13W7W14

The driverSends the named drivers’ records to eRODS in 30-day increments (W17), then reads them the way the inspector will (W18) before anyone arrives.

What the ELD recordsEvery transfer with its receipt, filed beside the roadside record on W14. W7 exports one driver, W13 packages the audit period.

How to read a non-visual row. Backend, hardware and certification requirements have no screen of their own. Where one is visible through a screen, that screen is named: a time-sync failure surfaces as the D19 diagnostic banner, and the Bluetooth contract is visible only as what D2 and D4 are able to show.

Where the web is complete. Every distinct web MUST lands on W1 to W18 with no gaps, and no W screen is missing. All six missing screens were on the driver side, where the shift happens and the record is made. One of them, D34, was found by walking the web app rather than the driver app: W16 promised an assignment would come back to the driver, and nothing received it.

Four

Gaps and Owners

Six screens, drawn as D29 through D34, and one promise that needed no new screen. Two close MUST requirements and are not optional. The rest close gaps between what the package says and what was drawn, and their shape has not been put to Tenna, so each ships with a question rather than an assumed answer.

D1

Biometric sign-in

Settled, undrawn Tenna · product

Why it has to existJose set it in the 28 August huddle, in the sentence that settled P2: the credential form, “with Face ID for the next time.” The form was drawn and the second half was not. No screen, data element, requirement or question mentioned it until now.

Still to answer

A device enrols one face, so on a shared cab tablet one look opens any driver’s session. Drawn as a personal-device capability that carrier policy can lock off on W15, with the passkey alternative recorded as Q24.

See the wireframe →

D29

Stationary prompt

Not optional Tenna’s FMCSA advisor

Why it has to exist§395.24(b)(2). Five minutes stationary in Driving and the ELD must prompt; 60 seconds without an answer transitions to ON. The requirement text calls defects here among the most common revocation triggers.

Still to answer

Confirm the window and the recorded origin before build. Built in the Phase 2 spike as HosPromptSheet.tsx, so this is a drawing gap rather than a build gap.

See the wireframe →

D30

Logout · end of shift

Not optional Tenna · product

Why it has to existThe driver-login requirement reads “login and logout”. Searching the 8/28 wireframes for logout or sign-out returns nothing: D24 has no such control and D4’s tab bar is Home, Logs, Inspect, More.

Still to answer

Does logging out release the truck? Sticky pairing (D2) exists to cut unidentified driving, and this is where the two rules meet.

See the wireframe →

D31

Inspection exit

Proposal Tenna · product

Why it has to existD15 gates entry behind a PIN and locks the session so an officer cannot leave the log view. Nothing drew getting the phone back, and D15’s own open question names a forgotten PIN at the roadside as the failure case.

Still to answer

What is the recovery for a forgotten PIN: a carrier reset from W15, a support code, or a timed auto-exit?

See the wireframe →

D32

Malfunction cleared

Proposal Tenna’s FMCSA advisor

Why it has to existD18 tells the driver to keep paper logs “from this point forward” and never tells them to stop. The malfunction-engine requirement asks for a clear event when the condition resolves.

Still to answer

Does the paper-log period end at the clear event or at the driver’s acknowledgment? An auditor will ask for both timestamps.

See the wireframe →

D34

Assigned driving

Proposal Tenna · product

Why it has to existW16 says it in three places: “assigning routes it to the driver to accept”, a data element reading “Assignment → driver, and the driver’s acceptance”, and an E3 sourcing line about telling an assigned driver they have something to confirm. The 8/28 flow-map note called it “the second push-driven round trip.” D22 runs outbound only and D20 is the log-edit flow, so nothing drew it.

Still to answer

What happens on a rejection? W16 has to reopen the record, and a second rejection needs somewhere to go rather than looping between the two screens.

See the wireframe →

D33

Active driver swap

Proposal Tenna · product

Why it has to existD3 tells the driver “swap who is at the wheel from the dashboard.” D4 draws status, clocks, vehicle, a certification nudge and a tab bar, and nothing else. Attribution of driving time depends on it.

Still to answer

May a swap happen while the tracker reports motion, or only at a stop?

See the wireframe →

What this walk did not settle. P3 waits on the retention answer from the compliance consultant. P4 is agreed in routing and not in content. Q7 and Q23 are hardware questions the walk touched but did not answer. None of them blocks the five screens above.

And one judgement call. The 8/28 flow map had a note deferring its redraw until the W18 scope call, Q20, landed. Q20 is still open. The map is redrawn anyway, because a map that stays wrong through two revisions costs more than a map that may need one more edit.
