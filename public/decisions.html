Owner
Q1

Duty status when the phone loses its link to the vehicle

ELD Compliance Blocking Phase 3

§4.4.1.2 requires that after 5 consecutive minutes stationary while Driving, the ELD prompt the driver, and that 1 minute without a response switch duty status to On-duty not driving. Our state machine only counts stationary minutes it directly observes — it will not attest to time it could not see.

The difficulty is that the most ordinary stop in real operation — the driver parks and walks away carrying the phone — is itself the loss of the link. The stationary window never completes, so duty status stays in Driving. The regulation assumes an ELD wired into the vehicle that never loses sight of it; a phone-based recorder does not have that property.

What makes this tractable: the tracker already records what we are missing. It emits BEGIN_STOP / END_STOP with timestamp, odometer and position, and stores them while no phone is connected. The evidence exists and is attested by the device that stayed with the vehicle — it simply arrives later. That turns the problem from "we cannot know" into "may we act on the device's attestation after the fact," which is a compliance judgement rather than an engineering one. 

Field evidence · 8 Aug · 87-minute session
  entered Driving 09:43:41 — and never left it
  3 engine shut-downs recorded while status stayed Driving
  10 connection losses, several exceeding ten minutes
What we need

1 · Which component is the ELD of record — the tracker, which stays with the vehicle and already logs stop/start, or the phone? Most deployments treat the hardware as the recorder and the phone as its display; if that is the intended model, it changes what the app owns.
2 · May the tracker's stored BEGIN_STOP timestamp serve as attested stationary evidence, letting the 5-minute window be evaluated retroactively on reconnect and the On-duty switch recorded at its true time?
3 · If retroactive duty-status changes are not permissible, what is the correct behaviour instead?
4 · Does this fall under §4.6 malfunction and diagnostic reporting rather than being purely a duty-status question?

Our position pending an answer We have not guessed. The machine holds Driving rather than inventing a transition it cannot support, which we believe is the safer failure. We would rather implement the correct behaviour once than ship a plausible one now.

Q2

Is ENGINEHOURS the ECM lifetime total?

Geometris Open

Item 0x11 behaves as a genuine monotonic engine-hour counter — it advanced 2.4 h → 3.0 h across 39.5 minutes of running time, 1:1 with actual run time, and carried across days and ignition cycles. But a 126,500-mile F-150 has thousands of engine hours, not three. The counter is evidently running from a device-local baseline.

What we need

1 · Is ENGINEHOURS specified as the ECM lifetime total, or as a device-local accumulator? The protocol document states only the ×10 scaling.
2 · If device-local, what resets it — installation, configuration, firmware update, power loss? Does it survive a reboot? We need to know whether a value can silently restart at zero mid-service.
3 · Can the device report the ECM's true total (J1939 PGN 65253, or OBD-II Mode 01 PID 0x7F) where the vehicle exposes it?

Engine hours is one of the four data elements named in SoW §3.1 and is required on FMCSA event records — this needs an answer before Phase 3. See also Q8; it may be specific to light-duty vehicles.
Q3

0 versus the unavailable sentinel for missing data

Geometris Open

ENGINEHOURS returns 0 whenever the engine is off, rather than the 0xFFFFFFFF unavailable sentinel that RPM uses in the same condition. The zero arrives precisely at engine shut-down — which is exactly when the §4.5.1 record is written. 

engine-power / code 3 (shut-down) · auto
  timestamp    17:37:07
  odometer     126,490.7 mi
  engineHours  0          ← indistinguishable from a real zero

A wrong-but-plausible number in a compliance record is worse than a blank one: 0 cannot be distinguished downstream from a legitimate zero, so nothing can flag it. An unavailable sentinel would let us record honestly. The same pattern appears elsewhere — ignition off, ODOMETER reads 0 and VIN returns an empty string.

What we need

1 · Can ENGINEHOURS use 0xFFFFFFFF when the ECM is not supplying a value?
2 · Is 0 the intended "no data" convention for ODOMETER and VIN, and which items use 0xFFFFFFFF instead? We would rather not infer this per-field from observation.

Q4

Unidentified-driving capacity, retention, and drain rate

Geometris Open

The device embeds one stored event at a time in the telemetry stream and repeats that event across 2–4 consecutive frames before advancing — 4–11 seconds per event. With a backlog of 46 events on 8 August, draining took several minutes, and the device redelivered the whole backlog on every reconnection.

What we need

1 · Why is each event repeated across multiple frames rather than advancing immediately — is there an acknowledgement mechanism we are missing?
2 · Is a bulk-transfer mode available that the document does not describe?
3 · What is the storage capacity, and what happens on overflow — oldest discarded, or newest refused?
4 · How long are events retained if never purged?
5 · Does the feature need explicit enablement per device, and is it enabled across Tenna's fleet?

Q5

No authentication in the BLE protocol

Geometris Tenna Open

The protocol as documented has no pairing, bonding, or authentication. Any phone in range that knows the service UUID can connect, read VIN, odometer, position and engine data, and issue the purge command that permanently deletes the device's stored unidentified-driving events. We confirmed connection with no pairing prompt and no credential of any kind.

What we need

1 · Is unauthenticated access the intended production posture?
2 · Is any pairing, bonding or application-layer authentication available or planned?
3 · Given purge is destructive, unacknowledged and all-or-nothing, should it be gated behind something stronger than proximity?

Q6

Which odometer is authoritative for compliance?

Tenna Open

The device's odometer reads consistently ~2.8 miles below the vehicle dashboard (126,488.8 vs 126,491.6). The gap is consistent with ordinary ECM-versus-dashboard calibration plus the protocol's whole-kilometre resolution — about 0.002% over 126,000 miles — and the value tracks distance correctly, advancing 16.16 mi over a measured drive.

This is not an app defect, but the two sources will never agree exactly.

What we need

Which value does Tenna consider authoritative for compliance records and customer-facing reporting?

Q7

Mapping a device serial to a Tenna asset ID

Tenna Open

The device exposes a serial number (item 0x0F) and its BLE MAC address. How does either map to a Tenna asset ID, and which is the stable production identifier?

Correction · 9 Aug

Addresses published before 9 August were byte-reversed — see Q14. WQ-87X060680218 is 78:42:1C:12:16:66, not the 66:16:12:1C:42:78 quoted earlier. Any lookup against the old value would have matched nothing, and would have looked like missing inventory rather than a formatting bug.

Q8

Fleet composition — light-duty OBD-II vs heavy-duty J1939

Tenna Open

All testing so far has been on a light-duty Ford F-150 over OBD-II. Several observations — particularly the engine-hours behaviour in Q2 — may be specific to that vehicle class. Heavy-duty vehicles on J1939 typically expose total engine hours directly.

What we need

What is the actual composition of the fleet this will serve? If it is predominantly heavy-duty, Q2 may not affect production at all, and we should retest on a representative vehicle before treating it as a gap.

Q9

Two phones connected to one tracker

Geometris Open

Untested and undocumented. What happens when two phones connect, or attempt to connect, simultaneously? Can both drain the stored-event queue? Can either purge it out from under the other? This matters for driver handover, and for a mechanic's phone connecting to a truck already carrying a driver's.

Q10

Firmware predating the TLV format (protocol 0)

Geometris Open

The app rejects protocol-0 frames with an explicit error rather than implementing the legacy fixed-layout format. Worth noting: the device broadcasts protocol 0 by default until the start command is written, so protocol 0 is the boot state, not necessarily older firmware.

What we need

What proportion of the deployed fleet would still require the legacy format? If it is zero, we leave the rejection in place permanently.

No open questions for that owner.

Settled

Four answered by hardware, not by assumption.

Each of these was an ambiguity in the protocol document that a literal reading would have resolved the wrong way — silently, with no error to signal it. They stay in the log with their evidence so the reasoning survives.

Q11

Control-word byte order

Geometris Answered 6 Aug

The document specifies control words as 0x0201 without stating wire byte order.

Answer

The device reads them little-endian: 0x0201 must be sent as bytes 01 02. Sending the big-endian reading 02 01 is interpreted as 0x0102 and produces no ELD data — silence, with no error. Worth flagging for the document.

Q12

Latitude / longitude sign encoding

Geometris Answered 6 Aug

The document's only worked example is east of Greenwich, leaving negative coordinates undefined.

Answer

Two's complement, confirmed. A US fix decoded correctly as 39.29537, −84.59589.

Q13

Notification cadence

Geometris Answered 6–8 Aug

Undocumented — and it determines whether the FMCSA engine-synchronisation requirement can be met at all.

Answer

Measured ~2.1 s while driving and ~2.8 s at idle (p95 3.1 s) — roughly twice as fast as the FMCSA 5-second requirement, and unchanged while the app is backgrounded. No watchdog retuning was needed.

Remaining sub-question: is the cadence configurable, and is it guaranteed under conditions we have not exercised (poor GPS, heavy bus traffic)?
Q14

Byte order of the device-address characteristic

Geometris Answered 9 Aug

Characteristic 0x2A59 carries the device address least-significant octet first — the order Bluetooth uses on the wire, but the reverse of how an address is written everywhere else. The document does not state the order, the same omission as the control words in Q11.

Answer · found on Android

Android reports a peripheral's advertised address directly, so one tracker produced two readings that are exact reversals: 

advertised address (Android)   1C:C3:AB:87:DD:3A
characteristic 0x2A59 read     3A:DD:87:AB:C3:1C

This was invisible for the whole iOS phase, and not for want of looking: iOS deliberately hides the hardware address behind a per-app UUID, so there was no second reading to compare against. The value looked well-formed and stayed stable — and was wrong the whole time. It matters because the address is a candidate key for tying a device to a Tenna asset record (Q7).

Hardware setup for Phase 2 validation

Adam Schaible (AppAxis) · Mark Leszczynski (Tenna)

Working session on what hardware the Phase 2 spike would need in hand to prove the broadcast contract end to end against real Geometris WQ devices, rather than against a simulator.

Outcome

Mark shipped 4 trackers and power supplies.

The power supplies matter more than they sound: they allow a tracker to be driven from a desk rather than only from a vehicle's OBD port. Bench testing on desk power has since carried a large share of the protocol work — BLE pairing, TLV parsing, the store-and-forward drain, background capture, and the Android foreground service were all validated without a vehicle, reserving drive time for the things that genuinely need motion.
