P3

Aug 20 session Open

The second-by-second readings from the tracker (position, speed, odometer) won't go through Tenna's existing data pipeline. So: does Tenna need to keep those raw readings at all? And if yes, does Tenna store and process them, or does the ELD hold them? The answer decides where exports, audit files, and any Tenna-side reporting on this data come from.

The candidates

A · The ELD keeps the raw data: Tenna gets the results (events, daily summaries, alerts) but not the raw readings; exports and audit files come from the ELD.
B · Tenna keeps the raw data: the raw readings flow into Tenna and are stored alongside its other asset data, through a new connection built for this.

Where it stands, 28 August

Still open, and it is Adam's to move. Tamar said that makes sense to her subject to Luke, and raised the governance angle. Adam flagged the practical constraint, that a packet every three seconds through a driver's personal phone is a real data cost over a month, and the regulatory one, that an audit may require the raw data to prove the ELD events were recorded properly. Q1

Duty status when the phone loses its link to the vehicle

ELD Compliance Blocking Phase 3

§4.4.1.2 requires that after 5 consecutive minutes stationary while Driving, the ELD prompt the driver, and that 1 minute without a response switch duty status to On-duty not driving. Our state machine only counts stationary minutes it directly observes; it will not attest to time it could not see.

The difficulty is that the most ordinary stop in real operation, the driver parks and walks away carrying the phone, is itself the loss of the link. The stationary window never completes, so duty status stays in Driving. The regulation assumes an ELD wired into the vehicle that never loses sight of it; a phone-based recorder does not have that property.

What makes this tractable: the tracker already records what we are missing. It emits BEGIN_STOP / END_STOP with timestamp, odometer and position, and stores them while no phone is connected. The evidence exists and is attested by the device that stayed with the vehicle; it simply arrives later. That turns the problem from "we cannot know" into "may we act on the device's attestation after the fact," which is a compliance judgement rather than an engineering one. 

Field evidence · 8 Aug · 87-minute session
  entered Driving 09:43:41 and never left it
  3 engine shut-downs recorded while status stayed Driving
  10 connection losses, several exceeding ten minutes
What we need

1 · Which component is the ELD of record: the tracker, which stays with the vehicle and already logs stop/start, or the phone? Most deployments treat the hardware as the recorder and the phone as its display; if that is the intended model, it changes what the app owns.
2 · May the tracker's stored BEGIN_STOP timestamp serve as attested stationary evidence, letting the 5-minute window be evaluated retroactively on reconnect and the On-duty switch recorded at its true time?
3 · If retroactive duty-status changes are not permissible, what is the correct behaviour instead?
4 · Does this fall under §4.6 malfunction and diagnostic reporting rather than being purely a duty-status question?

Our position pending an answer We have not guessed. The machine holds Driving rather than inventing a transition it cannot support, which we believe is the safer failure. We would rather implement the correct behaviour once than ship a plausible one now.

Q2

Is ENGINEHOURS the ECM lifetime total?

Geometris Open

Item 0x11 behaves as a genuine monotonic engine-hour counter: it advanced 2.4 h → 3.0 h across 39.5 minutes of running time, 1:1 with actual run time, and persisted across days and ignition cycles. But a 126,500-mile F-150 has thousands of engine hours, not three. The counter is evidently running from a device-local baseline.

What we need

1 · Is ENGINEHOURS specified as the ECM lifetime total, or as a device-local accumulator? The protocol document states only the ×10 scaling.
2 · If device-local, what resets it: installation, configuration, firmware update, power loss? Does it survive a reboot? We need to know whether a value can silently restart at zero mid-service.
3 · Can the device report the ECM's true total (J1939 PGN 65253, or OBD-II Mode 01 PID 0x7F) where the vehicle exposes it?

Engine hours is one of the four data elements named in SoW §3.1 and is required on FMCSA event records; this needs an answer before Phase 3. See also Q8; it may be specific to light-duty vehicles.
Q3

0 versus the unavailable sentinel for missing data

Geometris Open

ENGINEHOURS returns 0 whenever the engine is off, rather than the 0xFFFFFFFF unavailable sentinel that RPM uses in the same condition. The zero arrives precisely at engine shut-down, which is exactly when the §4.5.1 record is written. 

engine-power / code 3 (shut-down) · auto
  timestamp    17:37:07
  odometer     126,490.7 mi
  engineHours  0          ← indistinguishable from a real zero

A wrong-but-plausible number in a compliance record is worse than a blank one: 0 cannot be distinguished downstream from a legitimate zero, so nothing can flag it. An unavailable sentinel would let us record honestly. The same pattern appears elsewhere: ignition off, ODOMETER reads 0 and VIN returns an empty string.

What we need

1 · Can ENGINEHOURS use 0xFFFFFFFF when the ECM is not supplying a value?
2 · Is 0 the intended "no data" convention for ODOMETER and VIN, and which items use 0xFFFFFFFF instead? We would rather not infer this per-field from observation.

Q4

Unidentified-driving capacity, retention, and drain rate

Geometris Open

The device embeds one stored event at a time in the telemetry stream and repeats that event across 2–4 consecutive frames before advancing: 4–11 seconds per event. With a backlog of 46 events on 8 August, draining took several minutes, and the device redelivered the whole backlog on every reconnection.

What we need

1 · Why is each event repeated across multiple frames rather than advancing immediately? Is there an acknowledgement mechanism we are missing?
2 · Is a bulk-transfer mode available that the document does not describe?
3 · What is the storage capacity, and what happens on overflow: oldest discarded, or newest refused?
4 · How long are events retained if never purged?
5 · Does the feature need explicit enablement per device, and is it enabled across Tenna's fleet?

Q5

No authentication in the BLE protocol

Geometris Tenna Open

The protocol as documented has no pairing, bonding, or authentication. Any phone in range that knows the service UUID can connect, read VIN, odometer, position and engine data, and issue the purge command that permanently deletes the device's stored unidentified-driving events. We confirmed connection with no pairing prompt and no credential of any kind.

What we need

1 · Is unauthenticated access the intended production posture?
2 · Is any pairing, bonding or application-layer authentication available or planned?
3 · Given purge is destructive, unacknowledged and all-or-nothing, should it be gated behind something stronger than proximity?

Q6

Which odometer is authoritative for compliance?

Tenna Open

The device's odometer reads consistently ~2.8 miles below the vehicle dashboard (126,488.8 vs 126,491.6). The gap is consistent with ordinary ECM-versus-dashboard calibration plus the protocol's whole-kilometre resolution (about 0.002% over 126,000 miles), and the value tracks distance correctly, advancing 16.16 mi over a measured drive.

This is not an app defect, but the two sources will never agree exactly.

What we need

Which value does Tenna consider authoritative for compliance records and customer-facing reporting?

Q7

Mapping a device serial to a Tenna asset ID

Tenna Open

The device exposes a serial number (item 0x0F) and its BLE MAC address. How does either map to a Tenna asset ID, and which is the stable production identifier?

Correction · 9 Aug · confirmed on the device

Addresses published before 9 August were byte-reversed; see Q14. WQ-87X060680218 is 78:42:1C:12:16:66, not the 66:16:12:1C:42:78 quoted earlier. Any lookup against the old value would have matched nothing, and would have looked like missing inventory rather than a formatting bug.

Verified directly on that tracker the same day. Connecting it from Android puts both readings in one session record, and they agree: 

advertised address (Android)   78:42:1C:12:16:66
characteristic 0x2A59, decoded 78:42:1C:12:16:66
The answer is more constrained than it looks · 2 Sep

This reads as a data-modelling question and is really a user-experience one. The pairing list has to name trucks before the driver picks one, and only the serial is available at that point: it rides in the advertised BLE name, on both platforms. The MAC address does not. Q14 records why, and it cost this project a month of reversed addresses: CoreBluetooth hides the hardware address behind a per-app UUID, so on iPhone there is nothing to read until a connection is open. Answering "the MAC is the production key" would mean connecting to every tracker in range just to draw the list, which is not a viable pairing screen. Unless there is a reason we have not seen, the serial is the only workable answer. What still needs confirming is cheap: that the advertised name carries the whole serial rather than just the WQ- prefix. The build matches on the prefix and reads the real serial from item 0x0F after connecting, so it has never had to rely on the name.

Q24

What a Face ID sign-in proves about who produced the record

Compliance Tenna Open

Jose asked for the credential form “with Face ID for the next time”, and P2 settled on that sentence. The ordinary way to build it is the way Motive and Samsara do: after a password sign-in, keep the refresh token in the device keystore behind a biometric gate. The operating system does the matching in hardware and hands the app a yes or a no. No face or fingerprint ever reaches the app, and none is stored or transmitted, which is what keeps this clear of Illinois BIPA and the state laws modelled on it.

The consequence is the part worth deciding in advance: Cognito sees a token refresh, not a person. A biometric unlock is evidence about who is holding the device, and only as good as whose face the device has enrolled.

Why it has to be decided rather than discovered

§395.32 puts a driver ID on every event, and §395.22(b) makes the carrier responsible for drivers using their own accounts. A phone enrols one face. On a driver’s personal phone that is good evidence. On a shared cab tablet, which the inventory has assumed since the June session, the enrolled face belongs to whoever set the tablet up, and one look would open any driver’s session. Team driving makes it plainer still: two drivers, one device, one enrolled face, so the co-driver types a password on D3 whatever D1 offers.

Our position is that biometric sign-in ships for personal devices and is locked off by carrier policy for shared ones, drawn that way on W15, and that the ELD records which sign-ins were biometric so an audit can tell them apart. The stronger alternative is passkeys, where the biometric releases a device-bound key that signs a server challenge and Cognito gets cryptographic proof of the device and the user rather than a refreshed token. Whether the pool can do that today is a question for Kumaran, who already has the Amplify flow written.

Raised from D1, where the promise was made and never drawn. Bears on D3, D24, D30 and W15, and on the offline grace P2 leaves open.
Q23

Which VIN is authoritative when the vehicle and the asset record disagree

Compliance Tenna Open

The same question as Q6, which asks it of the odometer; nobody had asked it of the VIN. Pairing resolves a tracker serial to a Tenna asset record, and that record lists a VIN. Once the engine starts, the tracker reports the VIN it reads from the vehicle. The two can disagree: a tracker moved between trucks and not re-provisioned, a record entered by hand, a replacement engine or ECM.

Why it has to be decided rather than discovered

The VIN goes on the ELD record, so this is not a display preference. Our position is that the observed VIN wins, because it is the only one describing what actually moved, with the disagreement raised to the carrier as an exception rather than silently resolved. But that has a cost worth stating: a mis-provisioned tracker then produces a compliance record against a VIN that no Tenna asset claims, and something has to reconcile it. The alternative, trusting the asset record, produces a clean record that is quietly wrong, which is worse.

Raised from D2, where the confirmation state makes the disagreement visible for the first time. Bears on D16 and every output file.

Q10

Firmware predating the TLV format (protocol 0)

Geometris Open

The app rejects protocol-0 frames with an explicit error rather than implementing the legacy fixed-layout format. Worth noting: the device broadcasts protocol 0 by default until the start command is written, so protocol 0 is the boot state, not necessarily older firmware.

Q15

How is a non-driver vehicle movement classified?

ELD Compliance Open

The 17 August review lists the ordinary operating reasons unidentified driving occurs: mechanic road test and maintenance, yard movement, OEM warranty work movement, and yard relocation, which may be a yard a mile away. It then notes that carriers try to cover these with an exempt-driver account, and that those accounts draw pushback because they are difficult to enforce.

The proposed answer is upstream attribution: a two-tap stamp (D27) that a named mechanic or yard operator applies before moving the vehicle, carrying a reason and, where Tenna's Maintenance module can supply it, a work order number. What that produces in the event record is the open question. The movement still generates engine and motion data the ELD must account for.

What we need

1 · Is a stamped non-HOS move properly recorded as an unidentified-driving record that arrives pre-annotated under §395.32(b)(2), or as a distinct event class?
2 · If it is a pre-annotated unidentified-driving record, does the carrier still owe it a review, or does the stamp discharge that duty at the moment it is made?
3 · Does a named-person stamp with a work order carry more enforcement weight than the shared exempt-driver account it replaces? The design assumes yes; that assumption is worth testing before it is built.

Q16

May a carrier auto-annotate unidentified driving that matches a pattern?

ELD Compliance Open

From the review: "There can be operations that have excessive UDS transactions due to operational issues. Specifically Short Haul Operations with no login to ELD." A yard that produces two hundred sub-mile records a week is a materially different product problem than one producing six, and no amount of screen design makes two hundred individual human reviews happen.

The obvious mechanism is a carrier-level rule: records inside a yard geofence, under a distance threshold, auto-annotated with a standing reason. Tenna is unusually well placed to do this, because it already maps its customers' yards and sites. The obvious objection is that blanket treatment of unidentified driving is exactly what enforcement pushes back on, which is the same objection the review raises against exempt-driver accounts.

What we need

1 · Is a rules-based auto-annotation defensible at all, or must every record carry a human disposition?
2 · If it is defensible, what makes it defensible: a tight geofence, a distance ceiling, a named approver on the rule itself, an audit trail showing the rule that fired?
3 · If it is not, what does a compliant workflow look like for a carrier generating hundreds of records a week? That answer may be "fix the login problem," in which case sticky pairing (D2) and the non-HOS move stamp are the whole remedy and the report is only for the residue.

Our position pending an answer W16 is drawn with bulk annotation, which requires a human to select and apply, and without any automatic rule. Manual bulk action is the conservative floor; the rule engine is additive if it is permitted.

Q17

What counts as "unaccepted," and what is the retention unit?

ELD Compliance Open

Two definitional questions that decide the data model, both raised by the same sentence in the review: the in-vehicle ELD is only to hold the last 8 days of unaccepted unidentified-driving events, and the carrier must retain unidentified driving records for each ELD for a minimum of 6 months from the date of receipt.

The word "unaccepted" decides the design. A record the driver rejected is resolved from the phone's point of view and unresolved from the carrier's. If rejection makes a record accepted, the in-cab list stays short and the host queue holds everything. If it does not, a driver who rejects a record keeps seeing it for eight days with nothing to do about it.

"For each ELD" is device-scoped language, and the host report (W16) is fleet-scoped. Retention measured per device, per vehicle, and per carrier produce three different purge behaviors, and purging a record early is the failure that cannot be undone.

What we need

1 · Does a driver's rejection move a record out of the in-cab 8-day window, or only a claim?
2 · Is the 6-month retention clock scoped per ELD device, per vehicle, or per carrier, and does "date of receipt" mean receipt by the host, or by the in-vehicle ELD from the tracker?
3 · Does the 8-day in-cab window run on the same calendar as the 8-day log window, or from date of receipt?

Our position pending an answer Drawn as: a record leaves the in-cab list once the driver has answered it either way, and stays open on the host until the carrier annotates or assigns it. Retention is modelled at the record level with the source device stamped on it, which satisfies a per-ELD reading without foreclosing the others.

Q18

Formal sign-off on the Telematics transfer election

ELD Compliance Tenna Open

On specification item D17 the review is unambiguous: "You either do a telematics transfer (WEB or Email) or you do a Peer to Peer transfer (Bluetooth of USB thumb drive) not both." The original wireframe offered all three routes, which spans both families and is not a shape a device certifies in. The 8/28 revision elects Telematics and removes the peer-to-peer path from the driver screen and from the native-module scope.

This is a self-certification declaration rather than an engineering preference, and it is effectively one-way: adding a USB or Bluetooth path later means re-certifying. It should be signed off before Phase 3 starts, not discovered during Phase 5.

What we need

1 · Tenna's formal election of the Telematics ELD type, on the record, before build.

Q19

Is the DOT audit export over-scoped?

As we read it, the concern has two parts. A bundle that ships everything the system holds invites an auditor into material they did not request, and a 2,341-log-day package across fourteen drivers is not the shape of an FMCSA work-paper request, which, per the same review, targets named drivers over periods of up to 30 days. Q11

Control-word byte order

Geometris Answered 6 Aug

The document specifies control words as 0x0201 without stating wire byte order.

Answer

The device reads them little-endian: 0x0201 must be sent as bytes 01 02. Sending the big-endian reading 02 01 is interpreted as 0x0102 and produces no ELD data: silence, with no error. Worth flagging for the document.

Q12

Latitude / longitude sign encoding

Geometris Answered 6 Aug

The document's only worked example is east of Greenwich, leaving negative coordinates undefined.

Answer

Two's complement, confirmed. A US fix decoded correctly as 39.29537, −84.59589.

Q13

Notification cadence

Geometris Answered 6–8 Aug

Undocumented, and it determines whether the FMCSA engine-synchronization requirement can be met at all.

Answer

Measured ~2.1 s while driving and ~2.8 s at idle (p95 3.1 s): roughly twice as fast as the FMCSA 5-second requirement, and unchanged while the app is backgrounded. No watchdog retuning was needed.

Remaining sub-question: is the cadence configurable, and is it guaranteed under conditions we have not exercised (poor GPS, heavy bus traffic)?
Q14

Byte order of the device-address characteristic

Geometris Answered 9 Aug

Characteristic 0x2A59 carries the device address least-significant octet first: the order Bluetooth uses on the wire, but the reverse of how an address is written everywhere else. The document does not state the order, the same omission as the control words in Q11.

Answer · found on Android

Android reports a peripheral's advertised address directly, so one tracker produced two readings that are exact reversals: 

advertised address (Android)   1C:C3:AB:87:DD:3A
characteristic 0x2A59 read     3A:DD:87:AB:C3:1C

This was invisible for the whole iOS phase, and not for want of looking: iOS deliberately hides the hardware address behind a per-app UUID, so there was no second reading to compare against. The value looked well-formed and stayed stable, and was wrong the whole time. It matters because the address is a candidate key for tying a device to a Tenna asset record (Q7).

Confirmed on a second device once the fix was in. WQ-87X060680218, the tracker in the test vehicle and the one whose address had been published backwards, now decodes to 78:42:1C:12:16:66, matching the address Android advertises for it exactly. Two devices, both exact reversals: this is the encoding, not a quirk of one unit.

Q21

Who owns the carrier-side surface for unidentified driving?

ELD Compliance Answered 17 Aug

The August 20 wireframes flagged this as an inventory gap for the session: rejected unidentified-driving records needed a carrier surface to reassign them, but no W screen owned unidentified driving. The choice on the table was to fold it into the logs review screen or add a screen for it.

Answer

Add one, and make it a report rather than a list. From the review: "There needs to be a host specific report that would have pop ups with the reasons suggested for ease of identification of the UDS. Carriers need to maintain the report 2-3 times a week or it gets ahead of them and is unmanageable." That is W16. Folding it into logs review does not survive contact with a 46-record backlog needing grouping, bulk annotation, a reason taxonomy, and its own 6-month retention clock. The review cadence is designed into the screen for the same reason: it is the thing that actually fails in practice.

Q22

How much log history belongs on the phone?

ELD Compliance Answered 17 Aug

The wireframes asked whether a driver should be able to browse past logs beyond the 8-day review window read-only, or whether longer history is web-only. The same question applied, more consequentially, to unidentified driving: the original D22 was drawn as though the phone were where the whole backlog got worked.

Answer

The regulation already draws the line, and the review draws attention to it: the in-vehicle ELD holds the working window, 8 days of logs plus the last 8 days of unaccepted unidentified driving, while the host holds the 6-month record. "D22 seems to look like trying to do it all at the mobile?" It did. So no deeper log browse gets built into the app, D22 is capped at 8 days of unaccepted records, and everything past that is a host surface. This is a useful general principle for the rest of the build, not just these two screens: if the question is "should the phone hold more of this," the answer is almost certainly no.
