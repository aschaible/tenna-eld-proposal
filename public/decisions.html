Q1

Duty status when the phone loses its link to the vehicle

ELD Compliance Blocking Phase 3

§4.4.1.2 requires that after 5 consecutive minutes stationary while Driving, the ELD prompt the driver, and that 1 minute without a response switch duty status to On-duty not driving. Our state machine only counts stationary minutes it directly observes; it will not attest to time it could not see.

The difficulty is that the most ordinary stop in real operation, the driver parks and walks away carrying the phone, is itself the loss of the link. The stationary window never completes, so duty status stays in Driving. The regulation assumes an ELD wired into the vehicle that never loses sight of it; a phone-based recorder does not have that property.

What makes this tractable: the tracker already records what we are missing. It emits BEGIN_STOP / END_STOP with timestamp, odometer and position, and stores them while no phone is connected. The evidence exists and is attested by the device that stayed with the vehicle; it simply arrives later. That turns the problem from "we cannot know" into "may we act on the device's attestation after the fact," which is a compliance judgement rather than an engineering one. 

Field evidence · 8/8/2026 · 87-minute session
  entered Driving 09:43:41 and never left it
  3 engine shut-downs recorded while status stayed Driving
  10 connection losses, several exceeding ten minutes
What we need

1 · Which component is the ELD of record: the tracker, which stays with the vehicle and already logs stop/start, or the phone? Most deployments treat the hardware as the recorder and the phone as its display; if that is the intended model, it changes what the app owns.
2 · May the tracker's stored BEGIN_STOP timestamp serve as attested stationary evidence, letting the 5-minute window be evaluated retroactively on reconnect and the On-duty switch recorded at its true time?
3 · If retroactive duty-status changes are not permissible, what is the correct behaviour instead?
4 · Does this fall under §4.6 malfunction and diagnostic reporting rather than being purely a duty-status question?

Our position pending an answer We have not guessed. The machine holds Driving rather than inventing a transition it cannot support, which we believe is the safer failure. We would rather implement the correct behaviour once than ship a plausible one now.

Where it stands, 9/10/2026

Still the Phase 3 HOS-engine blocker, and now stated as the two scenarios that produce it. Planned: the driver parks, walks into a building with the phone, the Bluetooth link drops within a few steps, the app never sees five stationary minutes so the prompt never fires, and the log sits in Driving until reconnect. The tracker meanwhile stamped the stop and the key-off. Unplanned: the link drops mid-drive, the truck keeps moving, and the tracker records that stretch as unidentified driving that the app drains on reconnect.
Three rulings needed from Tom Cuthbertson: may the app close the Driving segment at the tracker's stop time automatically, or must it end at reconnect with an annotation, or be left to the carrier's "end drive at power-down" edit; may drained records that fill the same signed-in driver's own gap be attached to that driver automatically; and does link-loss time count toward the 30-minutes-in-24-hours malfunction clock in 4.6.1.1 when the tracker kept recording.

Q2

Is ENGINEHOURS the ECM lifetime total?

Geometris Open

Item 0x11 behaves as a genuine monotonic engine-hour counter: it advanced 2.4 h → 3.0 h across 39.5 minutes of running time, 1:1 with actual run time, and persisted across days and ignition cycles. But a 126,500-mile F-150 has thousands of engine hours, not three. The counter is evidently running from a device-local baseline.

What we need

1 · Is ENGINEHOURS specified as the ECM lifetime total, or as a device-local accumulator? The protocol document states only the ×10 scaling.
2 · If device-local, what resets it: installation, configuration, firmware update, power loss? Does it survive a reboot? We need to know whether a value can silently restart at zero mid-service.
3 · Can the device report the ECM's true total (J1939 PGN 65253, or OBD-II Mode 01 PID 0x7F) where the vehicle exposes it?

Engine hours is one of the four data elements named in SoW §3.1 and is required on FMCSA event records; this needs an answer before Phase 3. See also Q8; it may be specific to light-duty vehicles.
Q3

0 versus the unavailable sentinel for missing data

Geometris Open

ENGINEHOURS returns 0 whenever the engine is off, rather than the 0xFFFFFFFF unavailable sentinel that RPM uses in the same condition. The zero arrives precisely at engine shut-down, which is exactly when the §4.5.1 record is written. 

engine-power / code 3 (shut-down) · auto
  timestamp    17:37:07
  odometer     126,490.7 mi
  engineHours  0          ← indistinguishable from a real zero

A wrong-but-plausible number in a compliance record is worse than a blank one: 0 cannot be distinguished downstream from a legitimate zero, so nothing can flag it. An unavailable sentinel would let us record honestly. The same pattern appears elsewhere: ignition off, ODOMETER reads 0 and VIN returns an empty string.

What we need

1 · Can ENGINEHOURS use 0xFFFFFFFF when the ECM is not supplying a value?
2 · Is 0 the intended "no data" convention for ODOMETER and VIN, and which items use 0xFFFFFFFF instead? We would rather not infer this per-field from observation.

Q4

Unidentified-driving capacity, retention, and drain rate

Geometris Open

The device embeds one stored event at a time in the telemetry stream and repeats that event across 2–4 consecutive frames before advancing: 4–11 seconds per event. With a backlog of 46 events on 8/8/2026, draining took several minutes, and the device redelivered the whole backlog on every reconnection.

What we need

1 · Why is each event repeated across multiple frames rather than advancing immediately? Is there an acknowledgement mechanism we are missing?
2 · Is a bulk-transfer mode available that the document does not describe?
3 · What is the storage capacity, and what happens on overflow: oldest discarded, or newest refused?
4 · How long are events retained if never purged?
5 · Does the feature need explicit enablement per device, and is it enabled across Tenna's fleet?

Q5

No authentication in the BLE protocol

Geometris Tenna Open

The protocol as documented has no pairing, bonding, or authentication. Any phone in range that knows the service UUID can connect, read VIN, odometer, position and engine data, and issue the purge command that permanently deletes the device's stored unidentified-driving events. We confirmed connection with no pairing prompt and no credential of any kind.

What we need

1 · Is unauthenticated access the intended production posture?
2 · Is any pairing, bonding or application-layer authentication available or planned?
3 · Given purge is destructive, unacknowledged and all-or-nothing, should it be gated behind something stronger than proximity?

Tenna Open

Q7

Mapping a device serial to a Tenna asset ID

Tenna Open

The device exposes a serial number (item 0x0F) and its BLE MAC address. How does either map to a Tenna asset ID, and which is the stable production identifier?

Correction · 8/9/2026 · confirmed on the device

Addresses published before 8/9/2026 were byte-reversed; see Q14. WQ-87X060680218 is 78:42:1C:12:16:66, not the 66:16:12:1C:42:78 quoted earlier. Any lookup against the old value would have matched nothing, and would have looked like missing inventory rather than a formatting bug.

Verified directly on that tracker the same day. Connecting it from Android puts both readings in one session record, and they agree: 

advertised address (Android)   78:42:1C:12:16:66
characteristic 0x2A59, decoded 78:42:1C:12:16:66
The answer is more constrained than it looks · 9/2/2026

This reads as a data-modelling question and is really a user-experience one. The pairing list has to name trucks before the driver picks one, and only the serial is available at that point: it rides in the advertised BLE name, on both platforms. The MAC address does not. Q14 records why, and it cost this project a month of reversed addresses: CoreBluetooth hides the hardware address behind a per-app UUID, so on iPhone there is nothing to read until a connection is open. Answering "the MAC is the production key" would mean connecting to every tracker in range just to draw the list, which is not a viable pairing screen.
