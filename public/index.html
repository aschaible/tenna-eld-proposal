Revision 8/28/2026

What changed, and why.

On 8/17/2026 Tenna's FMCSA compliance advisor reviewed the package specifically for host reporting and sent back a memo. Four of its points change screens; two add them. Everything below is marked in place and outlined, so the edits can be found without hunting. The control at the bottom right switches between the original and this revision. Nothing was deleted from the original; it is still readable in full.

D22

Unidentified driving shrinks on the phone. The memo asked why D22 looked like it was "trying to do it all at the mobile." The in-cab device only has to carry the last 8 days of unaccepted records; review, annotation, and assignment are the carrier's legal duty. D22 is now an 8-day claim/reject list with a reason picker (D28), and the real work moves to a new host report (W16).

W16

New: the host unidentified-driving report. The memo's central ask. A working queue with the suggested-reason pop-ups, 6-month retention, and a review-cadence nudge, because a carrier that does not work it two or three times a week loses control of it. This also closes the gap the original page flagged for Thursday.

D17

One transfer family, not both. Per the memo's note on D17 of the specification: a device does telematics transfer (web services or email) or peer-to-peer (Bluetooth / USB), never both. The Bluetooth·USB option is gone from the driver screen and from the native-module scope.

D16

Three fields added to the inspection display. Sequence number, elapsed engine hours, and accumulated vehicle miles, per §4.5.1.1. FMCSA's own example omits them; the memo's experience is that including them is better received. The same fields drive the new host inspector view (W18).

D27

New: a non-HOS move stamp. The memo lists the four ordinary causes of unidentified driving (mechanic road test, yard movement, OEM warranty movement, yard relocation), and notes that carriers try to cover them with a shared "exempt driver" account that is hard to enforce. D27 lets a named mechanic or yard hand stamp the move in two taps, so the record never becomes unidentified in the first place.

W17 · W18

New: host eRODS transfer and host inspector mode. FMCSA now requests eRODS transfers days before an on-site audit, for named drivers over up to 30 days. W17 does that from the back office and refuses to push six months at once. W18 is the internal-audit log view the memo recommends.

Also changed: D2 keeps pairing sticky so short-haul drivers stop generating unidentified records by never signing in; D6 drops the shared-exempt-driver assumption; D9 settles at 8 days on the phone with longer history staying on the host; W13 carries a new open question about whether the audit package is over-scoped; W15 gains the reason taxonomy, the review-cadence reminder, and non-HOS move roles. The data element table and the flow map pick up the new surfaces.

Not changed, deliberately: the five product decisions. Nothing in the memo settles P1–P5, and the new host screens do not depend on them except where tagged.

P3

D27 · W15 · W16

D16 · D17

No signal means the phone leaves the cab. Telematics only is the rule, not a recommendation. With no signal nothing transfers by any method, so the driver hands the phone to the officer in inspection mode. D17 shows that path. D16 is drawn as the screen the officer holds.

Engine hours · VIN

Where two numbers come from. Engine hours come from the engine through the tracker. If the engine reports none, the tracker's own count is the fallback. GPS is never allowed. The VIN comes from the vehicle and wins any mismatch. W11 and W12 gain flags for both.

W4 · D29

Power down and idle shutdown. An idle shutdown is an engine power-down event and does not change duty status. A drive segment left open past key-off can be ended at the power-down time from the host, per FMCSA guidance. W4 gains that action. The driver gets no button for it.

D29 · Q1

Link loss does not change duty status. Tom: if the driver walks off with the phone while in Driving and never changes to On Duty, they stay in Driving. D22

A driver who says not mine stopsA driver who says Not Mine stops seeing it, but the next driver to sign in is asked again, until someone accepts it or eight days pass. D1 · Q24

Biometric sign-in is back to open. Tom would not sign off Face ID as the way into the ELD app itself. FMCSA wants the driver to hold a user code and password tied to the host account. He is asking his agency contact for a yes or no. D1 keeps the drawing with the toggle marked pending.

D3 · D33

Co-driver: both signed in, on one device. Applied from the 9/8/2026 call and reaffirmed today, and required by Appendix A 4.1.4(b). D35 · D2

Motion is speed, and pairing needs a login. The trigger is road speed above 5 mph, not a duration. Since a driver must be signed in to pick a tracker, "paired but not signed in" cannot happen. When a session has expired the app does not pair. Crew members who are not signed in are the carrier's operational problem, and they catch up with their own on and off duty edits.

W13

Audit export is the output file, only. The regulation defines what an auditor gets: one records file per driver. W13 becomes pick drivers, pick dates, generate, in 30-day increments through the same generator as W17. The checkbox bundle is gone.

Also from the call: a driver has up to one minute after login for the ELD to be fully operational, so the recommended order in the driver manual is key on, then log in. The 30-minute malfunction threshold is cumulative missing data in 24 hours, not consecutive. Two test cases Tom asked for, Bluetooth interference and other Bluetooth devices in the cab, are in the test plan.

Not applied: nothing from the call is held back this time.

The ownership split

Ownership and API Surfaces

A draft of which system supplies and owns each piece of data. The ground rule is Tamar’s: “you’ll have your own schema in the database, your own APIs, and you’ll structure them separately”, with shared users the exception. Nothing in this section requires access to Tenna’s schema.

Axis one

Origin

Where the value is first produced: measured by the tracker, stored in Tenna, typed by a person, or calculated by the ELD. Origin says nothing about who stores the result.

Tenna platform Tracker Entered Derived

Entered covers anything a person types on one of these screens: driver, mechanic, or back-office supervisor.

Axis two

System of record

Which system is the official store: it keeps the value and hands it over in an audit. A value can come from the tracker and still be owned by the ELD, and several are.

Tenna ELD Open

Earlier revisions of this page used one tag for both questions. That is why four rows used to read "Tenna API and ELD-owned" without saying which system owned what.

Four rules behind the split

  1. Tenna IDs are the join keys. The ELD stores a reference and reads the record when it needs to display it. It never copies a Tenna-owned record into its own schema, so there is no second list of people to keep in sync. Tamar, 8/28/2026: the users are shared, the rest is on your schema, and the ID is what identifies it.
  2. On-demand reads, not bulk sync. Screens fetch data when they need it. Nothing is copied on a schedule, so the ELD never holds a stale copy of a person or an asset. Working assumption from the 8/13/2026 kickoff.
  3. New, purpose-built APIs rather than direct access to Tenna's existing ones. Every T surface below would be built for this integration and scoped to what the ELD needs. Working assumption from the 8/13/2026 kickoff.
  4. The record that gets certified stays in the ELD's own database. Tenna can receive copies, and for events it should. But the log an inspector reads, its edit history, and the records files generated from it all live in one system. This is why S1 through S4 are kept separate.
